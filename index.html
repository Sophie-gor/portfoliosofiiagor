My background spans robotics, game payments, logistics and event agencies. It is not a straight line, and I treat that as my biggest strength. The common thread is the same in every role: learn the product quickly, explain it clearly, and keep the brand consistent wherever it appears.

Case: CompassNet

CompassNet · SeoulDigital Marketing Specialist (promoted from intern)Aug 2024 – Jan 2025InstagramLinkedInCopywritingPRVideoCanva

At CompassNet I handled marketing for three sister brands in the payments sector: Terminal3, Paymentwall and FasterPay. Terminal3, a game-payments company, was my main account.

My role

Digital Marketing Specialist in Seoul, promoted from an internship. I was the only person on digital marketing worldwide and handled all marketing in Korea. Around me were two event marketers abroad and a marketing manager abroad who reviewed the work I reported. Terminal3 was my main account.

What I did

Ran Instagram and LinkedIn for all three brands on my own, mainly Terminal3, and wrote the captions: event announcements, speaker promotions, recaps and holiday posts, all within one visual system. On the PR side I wrote the news articles for the brands and sent them to newspapers. Proposed the unicorn mascot that now appears across Terminal3’s photos and videos. Took and edited event photos, contributed to explainer videos, made the Book of Magic partner gift from start to finish (design, text and photo editing) and refreshed FasterPay’s feed. Tracked account performance for all three brands and worked on the annual marketing report; headline results are in the results block of this case.

What it shows

Event-led content follows the same cycle everywhere. At CompassNet I ran it: announce before the event, cover it on site and recap afterwards, in several languages. The copy followed a fixed structure: where we are, what a visitor can expect and how to book a meeting. I worked on both Instagram and LinkedIn, so I can write in a professional voice as well as a consumer-facing one. I have also explained a technical product in short video. I did this as the only digital marketer in the company, so planning, writing, designing, publishing and reporting were all in my hands, with a manager reviewing the results.

Influencers & PR

I have not yet run a paid influencer campaign from brief to report. Below is the experience I do have, and how I would run one for a new brand.

What I’ve done

  • StoryTaco: analysed foreign markets, searched for influencers suited to collaboration and contacted them. My work covered search and outreach.
  • R:ED: two years as the public face of a brand, inviting schools and brands to collaborate.
  • CompassNet: all marketing in Korea for three fintech brands, including localised PR: I wrote the news articles, sent them to newspapers and supported offline events.

How I’d run it

  1. Find: creators and experts with credible audiences in the brand’s field, in Korea and in each target market.
  2. Vet: review past brand partnerships and claims, and follow each market’s advertising rules.
  3. Brief: value first. Scripts approved by the company, no unverified claims and clear disclosure of paid partnerships.
  4. Measure: saves, shares and qualified enquiries (leads), in addition to views.

Concept ideas

Six concepts I would propose based on the trends today: three for consumers (B2C) and three for business buyers (B2B). They are my own ideas, not any company’s content, and the layouts below are quick sketches. Each is a format that adapts to any industry: the topic changes, the structure stays. B2C and B2B work differently, so each group has its own tone, channel and goal.

For consumers, B2C

Short, human and visual. The aim is trust, community and one clear next step, mostly on Instagram and short video.

B2C 1 · Series

Myth or fact?

“Posting every day always grows your reach.”

A common claim, answered by an expert

  1. The claim, in quotes
  2. Expert’s answer on camera or in a quote card
  3. One takeaway line

One claim per post in a repeated layout, 1080 × 1350. Answers come only from a named expert. Goal: trust and shares.

B2C 2 · Reel or carousel

From idea to launch

How a product is made and checked.

Behind the scenes

  1. Idea and design
  2. Quality checks
  3. Final approval
  4. Launch day

Uses footage and facts approved by the company. It reflects my interest in how products are made. Goal: a closer link between people and the brand.

B2C 3 · Reel

Ask the expert

One question, one answer, 30 seconds.

Followers ask, a specialist answers

  1. Question on screen, 3 s
  2. Specialist’s answer, about 20 s
  3. Captions in two languages
  4. End card with the product line

The same format repeats weekly, so it can be filmed in batches. Goal: community and repeat viewers.

For business buyers, B2B

Evidence, expertise and relationships. Decisions take longer and involve several people, so the aim is credibility and qualified leads, mostly on LinkedIn and at events.

B2B 1 · LinkedIn carousel

Problem, solution, result.

Slide 1 of 5

A customer story in five slides

  1. Hook: the result in one line
  2. The customer and their problem
  3. The solution, in plain words
  4. The result, with one number
  5. A quote and a next step

Canva or Figma, 1080 × 1350. Numbers and quote approved by the customer and the company. Goal: credibility and qualified leads.

B2B 2 · Series

3 numbers that matter

One data point per post, with its source.

Industry data, explained for decision-makers

  1. One number, shown large
  2. What it means for the business
  3. Source and date
  4. Link to the full report or a meeting request

One chart per post, 1080 × 1350. Only sourced, verifiable data. Goal: expertise and qualified leads.

B2B 3 · Reel

Booth in 15 seconds

A show recap in five shots.

Event recap for partners and buyers

  1. Booth wide shot, 3 s
  2. Product close-up, 3 s
  3. Team demo, 3 s
  4. Meeting or handshake, 3 s
  5. End card: “See you at the next show”, 3 s

1080 × 1920, captions in English and the local language, with music cleared for use. Based on my Terminal3 event work. Goal: follow-up conversations after the show.

Advertising rules and claims differ by industry and market. Every post above would need sign-off from the company’s legal and brand team before publishing.
